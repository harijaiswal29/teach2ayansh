            Likely exam questions (with answers)

            1. What is a habitat? The natural home of an animal, where it finds food, water, air and shelter.
            2. What are adaptations? Special features that help animals survive in their habitat. Three types: body, food and behaviour.
            3. What is camouflage? Using body colour to hide in the surroundings, like the Arctic fox in snow or the stick insect that looks like a twig.
            4. What is mimicry? Looking like another (dangerous) animal to stay safe, like the Viceroy butterfly and the Monarch.
            5. Herbivores (cow, elephant): flat teeth. Carnivores (lion, tiger): sharp teeth. Omnivores (bear, pig): sharp and flat teeth. Insectivores (anteater, frog): long tongues.
            6. Beaks: short, hard (sparrow, pigeon, seeds); strong, curved (eagle, vulture, meat); broad, flat (duck, swan, food from water); long, thin (hummingbird, honey eater, nectar); long, sharp (kingfisher, pelican, fish).
            7. Migration: moving to another place for food or warm weather (Siberian crane comes to India in winter; whales swim to warm seas to give birth). Hibernation: a long sleep in cold weather to save energy (bears in dens, frogs in mud).
            8. Skunk sprays a bad smell; pufferfish blows up to look big and dangerous.
            9. Humans: sweat cools us, skin protects from sunlight, nose and eyelashes keep dust out. Animals are born with their adaptations; humans use thinking and actions to adapt.

            Notes and small choices

            • Page 31 says the kingfisher and pelican have long, sharp beaks “to filter food from water”. That looks like a slip (it repeats the duck line), and the book's own page 33 riddle says “a long, sharp beak… to catch fish from water”, so the game teaches “to catch fish”.
            • Page 37 question 1b has two blanks (“People in hot places eat ___ and ___ food”), but page 36 only says “juicy food”. The game asks for “juicy”. Please check the second word with his teacher (it may be “light” or “cool”).
            • Extras not in the book: the camel and penguin in the habitat sort, the kitchen-tool comparisons for beaks, the cat that puffs up its fur, and the “make your own animal” choices. The kitchen tools are only memory aids; the exam answers use the book's words.
            • Open questions (Which animal would you choose to be? the Beak Test prediction, the Creative Hunt) accept any answer.
