                📒 My revision notes

                Read these before a test. Say each answer out loud, then cover it and try again.

                CommunityA group of people who live, work and help one another in the same place.

                Urban communityA city. Tall buildings, many people living close together, buses, metro, many kinds of jobs.

                Rural communityA village. Fields, fewer houses, less traffic and noise. Life is closely connected to the land.

                Metropolitan cityA very big city, like Mumbai or Delhi, with many smaller towns around it, all connected.

                Specialised jobOne special kind of work, like doctor, teacher or bus driver. Most city people do specialised jobs.

                Market dayThe day in a village when farmers bring fruits and vegetables to sell.

                PHCPrimary Health Centre: a small health centre near a village.

                ASHA workerHelps village families with vaccinations and medicines for common illnesses.

                Solar energyElectricity made from sunlight with solar panels, like in Dharnai village, Bihar.

                InterdependenceVillages and cities depend on one another. Food goes to cities; goods and services come to villages.

                NalandaA great centre of learning in Bihar about 1,500 years ago. Now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

                PollutionDirty air (air pollution) and loud noise (noise pollution) make city life less comfortable.

                Questions that often come in tests

                1. What is a community?A community is a group of people who live, work and help one another in the same place.
                2. What is a rural community?A rural community is a village. Life there is closely connected to the land, and many people earn their living through farming.
                3. What is an urban community?An urban community is a city. Many people live close together and do different, specialised jobs.
                4. Name two challenges of village life.The hospital may be far away. Farming depends on rain. (Also: water can be hard to get; children may walk far to school.)
                5. Name two challenges of city life.Traffic makes travelling slow. Air and noise pollution make life less comfortable. (Also: fewer trees and open spaces.)
                6. How are villages and cities connected?Food travels from villages to cities. Goods and services, like medicines, tools and clothes, travel from cities to villages. This is called interdependence.
                7. Why does a city need a metro?Many people live in a city and travel long distances every day, and the roads are crowded.
                8. How did Dharnai get electricity?The villagers worked together and set up solar panels that make electricity from sunlight.

                Part 1 · What is a community? (pages 7–9)

                1. All Aboard! (page 7): the goods train from a village to a city; what it carries (food) and what happens if it never arrives; “communities may look different, but they are all connected”.
                2. What Is a Community? (pages 8–9): the station example, the definition (a group of people who live, work and help one another in the same place), family, school and street as communities, busy versus quiet, Wonder Junction (any answer), and spelling “community”.

                Part 2 · The city (pages 9–11)

                1. The City (pages 9–10): urban community, what the city looks like, metropolitan city (Mumbai, Delhi), busy and fast-paced life, spelling “urban”.
                2. Everyone Has a Job (pages 10–11): specialised jobs, Kabir's community, and Wonder Junction: everyone earns money and buys what others make, so we depend on one another.

                Part 3 · The village (pages 11–17)

                1. The Land Changes (pages 11–12): what changes from city to village.
                2. The Village (pages 12–14): rural community, its features, Vrinda's morning in order, life connected to the land, farming and new village jobs, market day, spelling “rural”.
                3. Challenges in the Village (pages 14–15): hospital far away, PHC, ASHA worker, ambulance, specialist doctors in towns; farming depends on rain; long muddy walks to school; ways of getting water; how neighbours help.

                5. Checkpoint 1 (pages 16–17): the three clues, sorting features and struggles, and the broken bridge.

                Part 4 · Food on the move (pages 18–23)

                Answers I chose (please check against what the teacher says)

                • Checkpoint 1, question 2: “animals on the road” is sorted as a feature of village life (the book's table has four rows on each side).
                • Checkpoint 3 comparison table: village = farmers, weekly market, less transport (walking, a few buses), challenge: hospital far away and rain; city = specialised jobs, supermarket or grocery store, many buses, metro and trains, challenge: traffic and pollution. The book leaves these open.
                • Open questions (Wonder Junction, Think Route, communities he belongs to) accept any answer. The broken-bridge question accepts working together, telling the village leaders (panchayat) and finding a safe way to school; “panchayat” is my addition.
                • Page 18 says food from “villages like Meena's”; I used Vrinda's, since Vrinda is the village girl in the chapter.
                • Extra examples not in the book: the tree and branches for a metropolitan city, two hands washing each other for interdependence, wood, fruit, honey and leaves as forest resources, one pipe for many families.
                • The L1 and L2 worksheets the book mentions on page 28 (on Argus) were not opened.
