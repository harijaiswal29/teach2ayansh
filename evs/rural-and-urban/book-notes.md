# EVS Chapter 7 · Rural and Urban Communities: book notes

Page-by-page notes (book pages 7–28) with the answers used in the Village–City Express game. Notes are in my own words; answers to open questions are suggestions.


**Page 7.** cover: goods train from village to city. Q: what is it carrying, what if it never arrived? (Embark)

**Page 8.** Journey village->city and back. "communities may look different, but they are all connected". What are Communities? railway station picture (tea stall, porter). Neighbourhood: homes, shops, roads, parks, schools. People share same place and depend on one another. DEF: A community is a group of people who live, work, and help one another in the same place.

**Page 9.** Family/school/street are communities. Large or small; busy & crowded or quiet & spread out; all help people live together. Wonder Junction: one place many people use every day, why important. "The City: An Urban Community" train reached city; picture: metro, bus, auto, supermart.

**Page 10.** Tall buildings, hundreds of families; roads with buses, cars, bicycles, auto rickshaws; shops open early; schools, hospitals, banks, markets close together. Urban community = city. Very large cities like Mumbai or Delhi = metropolitan cities. DEF: A metropolitan city is a very big city, many many people live and work there, lots of smaller towns around it all connected. Many people live close together. Most people do SPECIALISED jobs (doctor, teacher, bus driver, office). All jobs important; city functions smoothly. Kabir: mother doctor, father office, neighbour drives bus, shopkeeper sells food, teacher helps children learn. People from many different places live together. City life busy and fast-paced; traffic, noise, pollution; parks, festivals, community spaces.

**Page 11.** Wonder Junction: doctor doesn't grow own food, farmer doesn't treat sick, bus driver doesn't build roads. How does everyone get what they need if each does one kind of work? (answer: they depend on each other / exchange, buy with money). "The Land Changes": train continues, city begins to disappear (picture: factory chimney, buildings -> fields, stream).

**Page 12.** Tall buildings smaller and farther apart; busy roads quieter; wide open spaces. Green fields, birds, stream, open sky. Community is changing. WJ: what makes this place different from city? "The Village: A Rural Community" small station (Surajpur), quiet platform, tea stall, dog sleeps in shade, green fields to horizon.

**Page 13.** Rural community = village. Fewer houses and buildings, less traffic and noise. People often know and help each other. Life simple and peaceful. Fewer schools, hospitals, shops, transport services than city. Vrinda lives here (picture: village market under banyan tree, "Clean market green village our pride", bullock cart, kirana store). Day begins early; before sunrise father working in fields; mother prepares breakfast; walks dusty path between houses and farms. Village life closely connected to the LAND. Seasons, weather, crops affect daily life. Many earn living through FARMING: rice, wheat, vegetables, fruits sent to towns and cities across country. Village life has changed over time.

**Page 14.** Today many villagers run small shops: clothes, mobile phones, groceries, furniture. Some are mechanics, tailors, teachers. Villages growing and changing like cities. Vrinda's village vs Kabir's city: neighbours know one another well; children play outdoors after school; trees, animals, ponds, open spaces. MARKET DAY: farmers bring fruits & vegetables to sell; families buy; small stores. Challenges in the Village: nearest hospital may be far. Small health centre or PRIMARY HEALTH CENTRE (PHC) nearby. ASHA worker helps families with basic health needs: vaccinations, medicines for common illnesses. Some villages ambulance. Large hospitals with specialist doctors (treat children, surgeries) in nearby towns/cities; travel long distance. Farming depends on RAIN: late/no rain -> crops fail, lose a season's work. Children may walk long distances to school; heavy rains make roads/paths difficult.

**Page 15.** picture: children splashing in rain on muddy road (sign: prathmik vidyalaya 2 km), farmer at cracked dry field. WATER challenge: wells or hand pumps; ponds or rivers; tankers; common tank; tap water too. Summer water dries up, walk far. Village communities work together: road damaged after heavy rain -> neighbours clear the way; water scarce -> families share; someone ill -> help reach hospital. "A community becomes stronger when people help one another."

**Page 16.** EcoRoute: Dharnai in Bihar had no electricity for many years; children studied by candlelight; people solved together; solar panels produce electricity from sunlight; Dharnai now known for using SOLAR ENERGY to power homes and community spaces. Q what people can achieve together.

  Checkpoint 1: Q1 How does each clue tell you this is rural? a. father goes to field before sunrise (farming / connected to land) b. market held only once a week (market day, fewer shops) c. walks through mud paths to school (no proper roads / fewer services).

**Page 17.** Q2 Sort: features vs struggles of village life. Features: fresh air, green fields, open spaces, animals on the road. Struggles: school far away, water hard to get, hospital far away, farming depends on rain. (animals on the road -> feature; book has 4 rows each so 4/4.) Q3 bridge broke after heavy rain; farmers can't take vegetables to station; children can't reach school. What should community do? (work together to repair, tell panchayat, temporary path, share carts...)

**Page 18.** The Train Loads Up: back at station, busier. Farmers carry sacks of rice, baskets of vegetables, crates of fruit; grown in villages like "Meena's" (book typo, should be Vrinda's). Vrinda's father brought sacks of rice. Workers load train. "Where is all this food going?" "To the city. People there are waiting for it." Back to the City: fields smaller, more houses, roads wider and busier, tall buildings.

**Page 19.** Train carrying vegetables, fruits, rice, grains grown by village farmers -> markets, shops, homes. Food Arrives in the City: workers unload at city station; TRUCKS carry food to markets across city; families buy. Kabir's mother buys tomatoes, onions, rice at vegetable market after work. Cities: open vegetable markets, small grocery stores, large malls with supermarkets/food sections. Some food came from villages like Vrinda's. 3-panel picture: 1 city railway station 2 city market 3 mall vegetable & grocery store.

**Page 20.** A Day in Kabir's City: lives in APARTMENT building. Father takes BUS to work; mother travels by METRO to hospital. After school plays in nearby park; library; weekends travel across city to visit relatives. City family BUYS food from shops instead of growing it. Water comes through PIPES into home. Hospitals, schools, markets close by and open every day. Because many people live close together, services are easier to provide.

**Page 21.** Life in an Urban Community: busy places, many live and work close together. Many different kinds of jobs: teachers, doctors, engineers, shopkeepers, drivers. Apartment buildings, hospitals, schools, parks, large markets. People from many different places -> lively and DIVERSE. Buses, trains. Picture: City Public School, City Hospital, Market, City Bank, metro, park. Challenges in the City: TRAFFIC makes travelling slow, roads crowded. AIR POLLUTION and NOISE POLLUTION make life less comfortable.

**Page 22.** As cities grow, fewer open spaces and fewer trees. Live close to thousands but may not know many neighbours. People work together to make cities better. Picture box "Cities have many challenges": traffic makes travelling slow; pollution makes air dirty; fewer trees and open spaces; people live close together but may not know their neighbours. Think Route: would you live in Vrinda's village or Kabir's city? one reason: jobs, food, transport, space, noise.

**Page 23.** Checkpoint 2: Q1 Write R or U: a farms and fields R; b tall buildings and malls U; c people mostly grow their food R; d metro trains and apartments U; e weekly market near banyan tree R; f supermarket open every day U; g children walk through fields to school R; h many different specialised jobs U. Q2 Kabir's city has a metro, Vrinda's village does not. Why does a city need a metro? (many people, long distances, crowded roads/traffic; village has fewer people, short distances). Q3 one good thing and one challenge of many people living close together (good: services easy to provide, hospitals/schools/markets close by; challenge: traffic, pollution, noise, crowding, don't know neighbours).

**Page 24.** The Invisible Thread: farmers in village grow food -> train carries food to city -> families in city buy and eat it. Connection works both ways: money earned from selling crops helps village families buy school supplies, medicines, tools, clothes - many made or provided in cities. Doctor who visits village may have studied in city. Medicine may be produced in city factory. Villages and cities depend on one another = INTERDEPENDENCE (bold). Picture cycle: farmers grow food -> train -> city families buy & eat -> goods & services from city (factory, medical store, school) help village families.

**Page 25.** Many Different Communities: not every community is village or city. Near the SEA: many people fish for a living. FORESTS: depend on forest resources. HILLS and MOUNTAINS: roads wind through steep slopes, weather cooler. Every community has its own way of life, strengths, challenges. All bring people together. Picture: "Communities are connected": food from villages goes to cities; goods and services from cities help villages.

**Page 26.** HeritageRoute: Nalanda: A Community of Learners. About 1,500 years ago Nalanda in BIHAR one of world's greatest centres of learning. Students & teachers from many parts of ASIA. Lived, learned, worked together; discussed ideas. Today ruins = UNESCO World Heritage Site; India long tradition of learning. Think About It: How is your school like a community?

**Page 27.** The Last Stop: travelled city->village->back. RURAL communities closely connected to the LAND; URBAN communities bring many people and services together in one place. Communities are connected. Food travels villages->cities. Goods and services travel cities->villages. People may live far apart but help one another every day, often without realising. That makes communities strong.

City: most people do SPECIALISED (different) jobs; buy food at a market / supermarket / mall / grocery store; transport is buses, metro, trains (many); main challenge: traffic / pollution.
