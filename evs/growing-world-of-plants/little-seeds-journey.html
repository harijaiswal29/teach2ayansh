            For grown-ups

            This follows EVS Chapter 4, “The Growing World of Plants”, in Ayansh's book (pages 7–22), page by page. Because EVS was hard for him last term, every fact starts with a short “💡” card that explains it with an everyday example (soaking moong, a straw, a raincoat, a sweater) and is read aloud before any question. Then he practises it the way a test asks it: fill in the blanks, true or false (with the corrected sentence), match the following, one-word answers, arranging in order, labelling the plant, and short answers built word by word. The last round of each stop is on your own: no clue until his second try. Each checkpoint stop is the book's own checkpoint, and the Grand Review is a 15-question practice test.

            Part 1 · Seed to plant (pages 7–9)

            1. Was the Tree Always Big? (pages 7–8): a seed grows into a tree slowly; the meaning of life cycle.
            2. The Life Cycle (pages 8–9): germination (water, air, sunlight; root down, shoot up) as a little experiment; seed, germination, seedling, adult plant, new seeds; the book's life-cycle circle in order; matching each stage to its meaning.
            3. Not Only Seeds (page 9, Detour): ferns grow from spores, ginger from an underground stem.
            4. Checkpoint 1 (page 9): the four blanks, “What is germination?” as a written answer, and spelling germination and seedling.

            Part 2 · Plant parts and their jobs (pages 10–14)

            1. Sprout Chaat (page 10): what sprouts are and what they give us, making sprouts in order, the chaat recipe, and the UN logo (leaves stand for peace).
            2. Plant Parts and Their Jobs (page 11): tap each part of a plant to learn its job, find the parts, match parts to jobs, and photosynthesis.
            3. Plants on My Plate (page 11): sorting the book's foods into roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds.
            4. Special Trees (page 12): the Bodhi tree and the zero-waste coconut tree.
            5. Water Travels Up (page 13): the coloured-water experiment, step by step, with a flower that changes colour hour by hour.
            6. Checkpoint 2 (page 14): label the plant, “Do plants sleep?”, and fruits with one seed or many.

            Part 3 · Plant homes and adaptations (pages 15–19)

            1. Homes of Plants (page 15): habitat and adaptation, and the five habitats with their weather.
            2. Desert and Mountain Plants (page 16): spines, thick green stems, waxy layer, deep or wide roots; cone shape, sloping branches, needle-like leaves, thick bark.
            3. Water and Rain Plants (page 17): broad waxy leaves, air spaces, flexible stems; pointed leaf tips, smooth leaves, large leaves, strong stems.
            4. Coastal Plants (page 18): mangroves' breathing roots and supporting roots, salty water, holding soil and stopping erosion.
            5. Checkpoint 3 (pages 18–19): the five blanks, sorting plants into habitats, and the three “answer the following” questions as written answers.
            6. A Library of Plants (page 19): the herbarium (Blatter Herbarium, Mumbai) and the word botanist.

            Part 4 · Caring for plants (pages 20–22)

            1. Care and Protection (page 20): what plants give us, do and don't, and a little garden to look after (not too much water!).
            2. Checkpoint 4 (pages 21–22): the pitcher plant picture, the five “choose the reason” questions, the answer questions, and seed → root → flower → fruit.
            3. Grand Review: 15 test-style questions from all four parts, new each time, and a Chapter 4 Plant Expert certificate that shows which part to revise.

            Answers to the book's checkpoints

            • Checkpoint 1: a. seed · b. seedling · c. fruits · d. root and shoot · Germination: the seed gets water, air and sunlight and starts to grow; a tiny root grows down and a shoot grows up.
            • Checkpoint 2: label roots, stem, leaves, flower, fruit.
            • Checkpoint 3: a. thick green stems · b. needle-like · c. broad · d. pointed · e. breathing · 2a. both help (deep roots find water deep down; a waxy stem stops water escaping), any one with a reason · 2b. cactus: thick green stem stores water, with a waxy layer; banana: strong stem stands in heavy rain and storms · 2c. air spaces in the stem and broad leaves.
            • Checkpoint 4: a. so they can grow healthy · b. so the leaves can make food using sunlight · c. so plants get enough space and food · d. it will weaken the plant · e. a botanist · 2a. spines, a thick stem that stores water, a waxy layer, deep or wide roots · 2c. in a herbarium · 3. seed, root, flower, fruit.

            Notes and small choices

            • The book says a seed needs water, air and sunlight to germinate, so the game uses that wording for the test.
            • Page 18's first line under “Coastal Plants” repeats the desert line by mistake. The game uses page 15's coastal line (salty air, sandy soil).
            • “Botanist” appears only in Checkpoint 4, so it is taught in Stop 16. The pitcher plant on page 21 is not named in the book; I named it.
            • Checkpoint 4 c: both reasons about weeds are true; the game marks “so plants get enough space and food”, the book's own words on page 20, and says the other one is true too.
            • Extra, not in the book: “Do plants sleep?” facts (flowers that close in the evening, tamarind leaves folding at night, no food-making in the dark); which fruits have one seed or many; that the Bodhi tree is a peepal tree; pressing a leaf in a book; neem water as a safe pest spray.
            • Open questions (the prediction, why logos use plants, your own hot-place plant) accept any sensible answer.

            Do it for real

            • Soak moong overnight and make sprouts with him (page 10). Look at the tiny root and shoot with a magnifying glass, then make the chaat.
            • Put a white flower (or a celery stick) in water with food colour and check it after a few hours (page 13).
            • In the kitchen, ask “which part of the plant is this?” for carrot, spinach, cauliflower, peas, rice and sugarcane.
            • Look at a plant in the morning and the evening (page 14). Cut open a fruit and count its seeds.
            • Before the test, read the “My plant words” card together and ask the checkpoint questions out loud.
