## Page 8 · Seed to Plant
- A big tree does not grow in one day. Most plants begin life as a tiny **seed**. The seed slowly grows and changes.
- All these changes are called the **life cycle** of a plant. The life cycle shows how a plant grows and makes new plants.
- **Seed**: a plant begins as a small seed in the soil.
- **Germination**: the seed gets water, air and sunlight and starts to grow. A tiny **root** grows down and a **shoot** grows up.
- Life cycle picture: seed planting → germination → sprout → seedling → tree → flowers → tree with fruit → fruit with seeds → (planted again).

## Page 9 · Life cycle (continued), Detour, Checkpoint 1
- **Seedling**: a small plant with a few leaves.
- **Adult plant**: grows bigger and becomes mature; may grow flowers and fruits.
- **New seeds**: flowers make new seeds, which grow into new plants, and the cycle starts again.
- Detour: not all plants grow from seeds. **Ferns** grow from **spores**; **ginger** grows from an **underground stem**.
- Checkpoint 1 answers: a. seed · b. seedling · c. fruits (with seeds) · d. root and shoot · 2a. Germination is when a seed gets water, air and sunlight and starts to grow; a tiny root grows down and a shoot grows up.

## Page 10 · Nutrition Junction, Wonder Junction
- **Sprouts** are baby plants that grow from seeds with just water and air. They give protein, vitamins and minerals.
- Making sprouts: wash moong or chana → soak in clean water overnight (8–10 hours) → drain next day → tie in a clean damp cloth or a covered box → warm place for 1–2 days → small white shoots = sprouts.
- Sprout chaat: wash hands, sprouts in a clean bowl, chopped vegetables, lemon juice, a pinch of salt and pepper, stir. Look for the tiny roots and shoots.
- Wonder Junction: the UN logo has leaves around the world map; the leaves stand for **peace**. Logos like Dabur and Tropicana use trees, leaves or fruits. (Open question: plants make us think of nature, health, freshness and peace.)

## Page 11 · Plant Parts and their Job
| Part | Job | We eat |
|---|---|---|
| **Roots** | hold the plant firmly in the soil; **absorb** water and **nutrients** from the soil | carrot, radish, turnip |
| **Stem** | supports the plant; carries water and food to all parts | sugarcane, asparagus, celery |
| **Leaves** | make food using sunlight, air and water = **photosynthesis** | spinach, lettuce, cabbage |
| **Flowers** | colourful, smell nice, attract insects | cauliflower, broccoli, banana flower |
| **Fruits** | grow from flowers; sweet, sour or juicy; carry seeds | apple, mango, jackfruit |
| **Seeds** | grow into new plants | sunflower, pumpkin, sesame, wheat, rice, corn, kidney beans, chickpeas, lentils |

## Page 12 · Heritage Route, Eco Route
- **Bodhi tree**: under it Gautama Buddha found wisdom and peace. Very old and sacred, over 2,000 years old. It teaches us to be calm, be kind, think deeply. Care: respect the tree, sit quietly, keep it safe.
- **Coconut tree** is a **zero-waste** tree: every part is useful. Coconut water to drink, leaves for mats or fans, trunk for wood, shell for bowls or toys.

## Page 13 · Discovery Route (experiment)
Big question: can water travel through a stem to the flowers? White flower (orchid) in a glass of water with food colour; stem cut at an angle; wait a few hours or a day. Result: the petals take the colour, so the **stem carries water up** to the flower.

## Page 15 · Habitats and Adaptations
- A **habitat** is the natural home of a plant. Different habitats have different weather, water and soil.
- Desert: hot and dry, very little water · Aquatic: in and around water, ponds and lakes · Mountain: cold, windy and snowy · Coastal: salty air, sandy soil · Rainy: wet and humid, lots of rain.
- **Adaptations** are special features that help plants survive in their habitat. They help plants get water, sunlight, air and space, and protect them from heat, cold, wind and animals.

## Page 16 · Desert and Mountain plants
- Desert (cactus, date palm): **spines** instead of leaves to save water; **thick green stems** store water and may make food; a **waxy layer** on the stem reduces water loss; roots go **deep or spread wide** to collect water.
- Mountain (pine, fir): **cone-shaped** so snow slides down; **branches slope downwards** so they do not break under snow; **needle-like leaves** reduce water loss and handle cold winds; **thick bark** protects from very low temperatures.

## Page 17 · Aquatic and Rainy-region plants
- Aquatic (lotus, water lily): some float; **broad leaves** catch sunlight; **waxy** leaves so water rolls off; **air spaces** in stems help them float; **soft, flexible stems** move with the water.
- Rainy region (banana plant, rubber plant): **pointed leaf tips** so water flows off quickly; **smooth leaves** stop water staying too long; some have **large leaves** to catch sunlight in thick forests; **strong stems** stand in heavy rain and storms.

## Page 18 · Coastal plants, Checkpoint 3
- Coastal (mangrove, coconut tree): mangroves grow **breathing roots** above the mud for air and **strong supporting roots** to stand firmly in soft soil; they live in **salty** water; they protect the shore by **holding soil** and stopping **erosion**.
- (The book's first line under "Coastal Plants" repeats the desert line by mistake. The game uses page 15's coastal line: salty air, sandy soil.)
- Checkpoint 3 answers: a. thick green stems · b. needle-like · c. broad (waxy) · d. pointed · e. breathing.

## Page 19 · Checkpoint 3 (questions), Trail Treasure
- 2a. Deep roots or waxy stems for dry places? Both help: deep roots find water far underground; a waxy stem keeps stored water from escaping. Any one with a reason.
- 2b. Cactus: thick green stem stores water (and makes food) with a waxy layer. Banana: strong stem stands in heavy rain and storms.
- 2c. Lotus floats because of the air spaces in its stem and its broad leaves.
- **Herbarium**: a library of dried plant samples (leaves, flowers, stems) kept for study, e.g. the Blatter Herbarium in Mumbai (plants from western India). Scientists learn by observation and experiments.

## Page 20 · Care and Protection of Plants
Plants give us food, oxygen, shade and medicines, so we must protect them. Water regularly but not too much; enough sunlight; remove **weeds** so plants get space and food; do not pluck leaves or flowers; protect from **pests** using safe methods; do not step on small plants. Cared-for plants grow healthy and strong.
