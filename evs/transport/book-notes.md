## p45 Embark
"How can we help everyone move better?" Photo: busy Indian road near school time (school bus, traffic policeman, zebra crossing, auto-rickshaws, bicycles, "SCHOOL AHEAD" sign, HONK!).

## p46
- Honk! Honk! The cars are moving slowly. The bus has stopped. Two bicycles are waiting. People want to reach school, work, the market and home.
- **Transport** helps people and things move from one place to another. We use cars, buses, trains, bicycles, boats, aeroplanes and many other vehicles.
- Transport creates problems too: too much **traffic**; smoke in the air causes **air pollution**; road may feel unsafe; places may be too far, too crowded, too steep, too difficult to reach.
- **Roads – the paths on which land transport moves.** A road is not only a path. Good roads need smooth surfaces, clear signs and careful rules.
- **How clever people solve transport problems**: every road, bridge, train, bicycle path and bus route began with a problem. Clever people do not only complain; they look carefully and try to solve it. Five simple steps:
  1. SEE the problem – What is going wrong?
  2. ASK why – Why is it happening?
  3. THINK of ideas – What can we try?
## p47
  4. TRY one idea – Draw it, build it or plan it.
  5. CHECK if it works – Did it help? Can it be made better?
  This is called **thinking like a designer** (caption: see, ask, think, try and check).
- **Managing traffic · The traffic signal**: a set of red, yellow and green lights used to control vehicles and pedestrians at road crossings. Invented to make roads safer and prevent accidents. **Red = stop, yellow = wait, green = go.** Help vehicles move in an orderly way and reduce traffic jams.
- Why were traffic signals invented? • To prevent accidents at busy intersections. • To control the movement of vehicles and pedestrians. • To improve traffic flow and reduce confusion on the roads.
- **The roundabout**: a circular road junction with a small island in the middle. Vehicles slow down and travel in the same direction around the circle until they turn toward the road they need. They keep moving smoothly, so they do not always have to stop and wait for a signal.
## p48
- Roundabouts help traffic move smoothly and reduce accidents. India examples: **Sion Circle and King's Circle in Mumbai, Hudson Circle in Bangalore, Panjagutta circle in Hyderabad, Yerawada circle in Pune.**
- Wonder Junction: If a roundabout helps vehicles move smoothly, why do some places still need traffic lights? Both help control traffic. Roundabouts can reduce waiting time, but **traffic signals work better at very busy intersections**. Both make roads safer and more organised. Find names of more roundabouts in your city (open).
- "Now let us travel to different places and see how people in other places solved transport puzzles."
- **Germany**: known for very well-planned highways called the **Autobahn**. A wide highway built for fast travel between cities. **No roundabouts or traffic signals.** Built to help people travel quickly between cities. Reduces delays because it has few stops and no traffic signals on the main highway. Helps traffic move smoothly and safely.
## p49
- Photo captions: A well-planned German highway with clear lanes and signs. / A safe cycle path near a school in the Netherlands.
- **Eco Route**: A bicycle runs on your energy. It makes no smoke. A street with more bicycles can mean cleaner air and quieter roads. (photo: CYCLE PATH in an Indian city)
## p50
- Wonder Junction: Many children in India enjoy cycling. What would make cycling to school safer? (open)
- **Colombia – The hill problem**: Some people live on hills; a hill can be very steep, so a road may be hard to build; a bus may take a long time to climb up and down. In **Medellin, Colombia**, many homes were built on steep hills; people needed to reach the city below for school, work and shopping; steep roads made the journey slow and difficult. "If the ground is difficult, why not move through the air?" The city built **cable cars**. A cable car is a small cabin that hangs from strong cables. It glides high above the houses and carries people up and down the hill. Today they help people reach the city quickly and safely; part of everyday travel. Caption: cable cars can be used as everyday transport in a hillside city.
## p51
- Trail Treasure: In some cities, cable cars are not only for tourists. They help people go to school, work and markets every day.
- Wonder Junction: When the ground is hard to cross, people may go up in the air, under the ground or over water. Can you think of another clever way to travel? (open)
- **Heritage Route · India's old moving puzzle**: Long ago, how could travellers, traders and messengers move across a very large land? Answer: the **Grand Trunk Road**, one of India's oldest road routes. Connected many faraway places like one long thread. A ruler named **Sher Shah Suri** improved and rebuilt it many years ago. **Trees** planted along parts of the road. **Wells** and resting places called **sarais** helped travellers. They could stop, rest, drink water and continue.
## p52
- The road helped people and goods move across long distances. Even today, parts of this old route are still used. Picture: milestone "Sher Shah Suri built Sadak-e-Azam (Grand Trunk Road), 16th century", bullock cart, sarai.
- Wonder Junction: Why would travellers long ago need trees, wells and resting places on a long road? (shade, water, rest)
## p53
- **Now it is our turn: India's moving puzzles.** Germany looked at waiting. Colombia looked at steep hills. India also has many transport puzzles. Look around your own town: you may see • too much traffic near school • honking on busy roads • smoke from vehicles • buses or trains that are very crowded • roads that feel unsafe for walking or cycling • people waiting too long for transport. Every place has its own problem, so every place needs its own clever answer.
- Discovery Route: with a grown-up, look near home or school, find one transport problem. Ask: What is going wrong? Who is facing the problem? Why is it happening? When does it happen most? What could make it better? Bring your observation to class.
