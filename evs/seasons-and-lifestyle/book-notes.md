## p31 — India's Six Seasons
- Long before weather apps, people in India watched the sky, birds, flowers, winds and fruits to understand seasons.
- The year is divided into **six seasons called ritus**:
  1. **Vasant** = spring — flowers bloom, air feels fresh.
  2. **Grishm(a)** = summer — Sun is strong, people look for shade and water.
  3. **Varsha** = rainy season — clouds bring rain, land turns green.
  4. **Sharad** = autumn — sky becomes clearer after the rains.
  5. **Hemanta** = early winter / pre-winter — air begins to feel cool.
  6. **Shishir(a)** = winter — mornings may be cold or foggy.
- Chart "The 6 Indian Seasons (Ritus)" shows them as a wheel in that order (1 Vasant … 6 Shishira).
- Each season has its own signs and mood; people learnt this by watching nature closely.

## p32 — Heritage Route: A Season Trail Across India
- India is big; the same season feels different in different places.
- **Hot places like Rajasthan**: loose cotton clothes; drink water, **chaas** (buttermilk) or **aam panna**; homes with shade and **thick walls** to stay cool.
- **Rainy places like Kerala or Meghalaya**: umbrellas, raincoats, **sloping roofs**; families eat hot, freshly cooked food during rains.
- **Cold places like Kashmir or Himachal Pradesh**: woollen clothes, warm food and drinks, homes made to keep people warm.
- Festivals follow seasons: **Holi comes with spring colours. Pongal, Lohri, Bihu and Makar Sankranti come around harvest time.**
- Wonder Junction: without a phone, how could you guess it may rain? Watch clouds, wind, smell of wet soil, birds or ants. (open)

## p33 — Meet a Climatologist
- A **climatologist** is a scientist who studies seasons and weather patterns over many years.
- **Dr. Roxy Mathew Koll** — Indian climatologist who studies how the **ocean and the monsoon** are connected. He studied ocean temperatures, winds and rains using **satellites, ocean buoys and weather stations**. He found the **Indian Ocean is warming**, which changes how the monsoon behaves (stronger or weaker rains in different places).
- A weather scientist studies the Sun, wind, clouds, rain and seasons; their work helps us prepare for heat and rain.
- Discovery Route: touch cotton, wool and something waterproof. Which is best for hot weather (**cotton**), cold weather (**wool**), rain (**waterproof**)?

## p35 — Clothes (cont.) and When Nature Changes, Food Changes
- **Cotton lets air pass through. Wool keeps warm air near the body. Waterproof material stops rainwater soaking in.**
- Find the fog patch, rain patch and strong Sun patch: each shows a weather problem; clothes are one way people solve it. "What is the weather telling me to wear today?"
- Food: we want cold drinks in summer and hot soup in winter — **our body feels the season**.
- **Summer** (cool down): mangoes, watermelon, buttermilk, coconut water, aam panna.
- **Winter**: **sesame laddoos and jaggery** — filling and warming foods.
- **Rains**: families eat **fresh, hot food because food can spoil faster in wet weather**.
- **Seasons help decide what grows. What grows helps decide what we eat.**
- Wonder Junction: what seasonal food do you enjoy in summer / winter / rainy season? (open)

## p36 — Seasons Café menus, Celebrations
- Q2: Who decides what is on a menu: chef, customers or weather? (open; the book's idea: the **weather/season** shapes it.)
- Summer menu: cool drinks (mango shake, lemonade, watermelon juice, ice-cream float), fruity treats (ice cream, fruit salad, watermelon bowl, mango cup), light bites (veg sandwich, corn cup, cucumber sticks).
- Winter menu: warm drinks (hot chocolate, masala chai, ginger tea, coffee), warm & tasty (tomato soup, veg clear soup, cheese toast, pasta), winter bites (roasted corn, peanut chaat, sweet potato).
- **When Nature Changes, Celebrations Change**: many festivals are linked to nature — a harvest, the first rains, spring colours, the end of cold days.
- **Holi** comes when spring brings flowers and colour. **Onam** is celebrated when the land is green after the rains and harvest time is near.

## p37 — Celebrations (cont.)

## p38 — When Weather Changes, Homes Change
- Rajasthan family vs Meghalaya family — would they build the same house? No.
- **Sloping roofs** help rainwater slide off in rainy places.
- **Houses on stilts** stay above floodwater in flood-prone areas.
- **Thick walls, courtyards and shaded verandas** keep homes cool in hot places.
- **Small cosy rooms and fireplaces** keep people warm in cold regions.
- **Steep roofs** stop snow piling up in snowy places.
- **Strong houses** withstand strong winds in windy coastal areas.
- A home is not just a shelter; it is designed to suit the weather and the place. Good builders listen to the place.
- Wonder Junction: building a school in a new place — three things to know about the weather (open).
- Picture panel: **Rajasthan Haveli** (thick walls and courtyard keep it cool in hot weather), **Kashmir Houseboat** (floats on lakes, special home in Kashmir), **City Apartment** (many families in one building; balconies and windows let in light and air).

## p41 — Eco Route: When Nature's Clues Change
- Nature has a rhythm: rains come, flowers bloom, mangoes ripen, birds return. In some places this rhythm is changing.
- Some summers feel hotter. Sometimes **too much rain**, sometimes **too little**. In cold places **snow and ice may melt faster**.
- Farmers may find rain comes late. Children feel hotter summers than their grandparents did. **Nature's clues are changing.**
- Pictures: **Dharnai Solar Village** (Bihar) — solar panels on homes and street lights.
- When seasons change in new ways, food, water, homes, clothes and festivals can all be affected.
- **We can help by planting trees, saving water, reducing waste, and not wasting electricity.**
- Every patch in the quilt matters; we can care for each one.
