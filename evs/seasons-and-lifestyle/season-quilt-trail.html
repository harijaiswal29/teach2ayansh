            ✏️ Ritu's exam tips

            • Read the question twice. Look for the key word: why, which, where, true or false.
            • Use the book's words. “Cotton lets air pass through.” “Wool keeps warm air near the body.”
            • Answer in a full sentence. Start with a capital letter and end with a full stop.
            • False? Write the correct sentence too. Teachers love that!
            • Learn the six ritus in order: Vasant, Grishma, Varsha, Sharad, Hemanta, Shishira.

            For grown-ups

            This follows EVS Chapter 8, “Seasons and Lifestyle”, in Ayansh's book (pages 29–44), in the same order as the book. Because EVS was hard last term, every stop teaches first: a “💡” idea card with a real-life example, read aloud, before any question. Every stop then ends with an ✏️ Exam corner in the formats exams use: fill in the blanks, true or false (with the correct sentence when false), match the following, one-word answers spelt with letter tiles, and answers built as a full sentence. The exam corner is on your own: no clue until his second try. A wrong answer costs a little, so three stars means very few slips.

            Part 1 · Nature's clues (pages 29–31)

            1. The Season Quilt (pages 29–30): the 12 quilt patches and the story in each, “nature changes, we change too” (food, clothes, festivals, homes), and nature's signs.
            2. Weather or Season? (pages 30, 32): weather can change in one day, a season stays for many weeks; the morning/afternoon/evening window; sorting; and the rain clues from Wonder Junction (clouds, wind, smell of wet soil, ants, birds).
            3. India's Six Seasons (page 31): the ritus wheel, Vasant (spring), Grishma (summer), Varsha (rainy), Sharad (autumn), Hemanta (early winter), Shishira (winter), with each season's sign; matching, putting them in order, and spelling.

            Part 2 · One season, many places (pages 32–33)

            1. Season Trail Across India (page 32): hot Rajasthan (cotton, chaas, aam panna, thick walls), rainy Kerala and Meghalaya (umbrellas, raincoats, sloping roofs, hot fresh food), cold Kashmir and Himachal (woollens, warm food, warm homes); Holi with spring, Pongal, Lohri, Bihu and Makar Sankranti at harvest.
            2. Meet a Climatologist (page 33): what a climatologist is; Dr. Roxy Mathew Koll, the ocean and the monsoon; satellites, ocean buoys and weather stations; the Indian Ocean is warming.

            Part 3 · We change too (pages 33–39)

            1. The Fabric Lab (pages 33–35): touch and water tests on cotton, wool and waterproof cloth (from the page 34 picture), which is best for hot, cold and rain, and dressing for the fog, rain and strong-Sun patches.
            2. Seasons Café (pages 35–36): our body feels the season; summer and winter foods; the café menus; fresh hot food in the rains because food spoils faster; seasons decide what grows.
            3. Festivals Say Thank You (pages 36–37): Holi, Onam, Pongal, Lohri, Bihu, Makar Sankranti; a festival as a thank-you to nature; his own family festival (Think Route).
            4. Homes for Every Weather (pages 38–39): sloping roofs, stilts, thick walls/courtyards/verandas, fireplaces, steep roofs, strong houses; a house builder for rainy Meghalaya and hot Rajasthan; the six homes in the pictures.

            Part 4 · Checkpoints and changing clues (pages 39–44)

            1. Read the Quilt (pages 39–40, Checkpoint 1): all five questions: clue to season, Finland and Chennai, very little rain, a patch's clue and people's response, and Picture A and B.
            2. When Nature's Clues Change (pages 41–42, Eco Route and Checkpoint 2): hotter summers, late or uneven rain, melting snow, Dharnai solar village, and how we can help. Checkpoint 2 asks him to talk to an elder.
            3. The Last Patch (pages 42–44): reading the quilt (mango = summer, sesame laddoo = winter, sloping roof = ready for rain), “that is wisdom”, and his own Quilt of Nature to draw on pages 43–44.

            Part 5 · Exam practice

            1. Key Words: 14 key words with meanings, a “which word means…” quiz, a word search and spelling.
            2. Practice Paper: a mini exam in five sections (A fill in the blanks, B true or false, C match, D one-word answers, E answer in one sentence). Every question is on his own. The questions change each time.
            3. Grand Review: 10 mixed questions from all four parts, new each time, and a Season Quilt Master certificate that shows which part to revisit.

            Notes and small choices

            • The book spells two seasons both ways: “Grishm” and “Shishir” in the text, “Grishma” and “Shishira” on the chart. The game uses Grishma and Shishira. Either should be fine in the exam; check with his teacher.
            • Open questions (I notice, I wonder, the café menu, his family festival, Ask an elder, My Quilt of Nature) accept any answer and then explain the book's idea.
            • Added to help understanding, not in the book's words: the December morning in Kashmir and Chennai, the report-card example for a climatologist, what each tool (satellite, buoy, weather station) does, the touch test results, the season and clues for each family festival, the Dharnai sentence (the book only shows its picture), the meaning of monsoon, and the house builder.
            • Checkpoint 1, question 2 (Finland and Chennai) and question 3 (very little rain) are open in the book. The game's answers are reasonable model answers, not the only ones.

            Do it for real

            • Be a nature detective: look out of the window in the morning, afternoon and evening, and fill in page 30 together.
            • Do the fabric test (page 33): a cotton handkerchief, a woollen sweater and a raincoat. Drop a few drops of water on each.
            • At dinner, ask: “Can you find a season clue on your plate?”
            • Let him ask Nani, Dadi or you the Checkpoint 2 question and write the answer on page 42.
            • Before the exam, ask him the revision sheet out loud and let him answer in full sentences.
