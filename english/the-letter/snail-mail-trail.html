              For grown-ups

              This follows English Chapter 4, “The Letter” (from Frog and Toad Are Friends by Arnold Lobel), pages 34–54 of Ayansh's book, stop by stop in book order. The story is retold in short, simple sentences in the game; the questions, word meanings and exercises follow the book. Every story stop has blue words he can tap to hear what they mean. The last round of each stop is an “on your own” round: the story is folded away and clues only come after a second try. A wrong answer costs a little, so three stars means very few slips.

              Part 1 · Read the story (pages 34–38)

              1. Under the Cover (page 34, Embark): guess what is under the cover, meet Frog and Toad, and guess what the story is about from its title.
              2. Toad's Sad Time (page 35): why Toad is sad. Words: porch, sighed, mailbox.
              3. Frog's Big Idea (page 36): Frog's letter, the envelope and the snail. Putting the parts of Frog's letter and the steps of sending it in order. Words: envelope, nap.
              4. Waiting at the Window (page 37): he taps the window to look for the snail with Frog; who said it? Word: silly.
              5. Four Days Later (page 38 and Real Route): how long the snail took, the whole story in order, and why a note makes us happy. Word: pleased.

              Part 2 · Think about the story (pages 39–41)

              1. Who Feels This Way? (pages 39 and 41): Questions 1 and 4. Answers: a. Toad, b. Frog, c. Toad; head down = sad, looking out again and again = hopeful, smiling = happy.
              2. How Long Was the Wait? (page 40): Question 2 (the very short to very long scale) and Question 3 (Snail Speed thinking).
              3. Pigeon to Phone (page 41, Heritage Route): pigeon, letter, telephone, mobile, smartphone.

              Part 3 · Word power (pages 42–48)

              1. Common or Proper? (page 42): the book's word search with its 8 clues. Common: freedom, telescope, astronaut, forest. Proper: Sundarbans, Himalayas, Ganga, Earth.
              2. This, That, These, Those (pages 43–44): near or far, one or many, sentences about the garden picture, and the “opposite” task.
              3. Where Is It? (page 45): position words, the book's door-stopper riddle, and making his own two-clue riddle.
              4. How Does It Look? (page 45): adjectives of quality (size, colour, shape, feeling, taste) and the book's four example sentences.
              5. How Many? How Much? (page 46): adjectives of quantity, the book's examples, and the koala / binturong comparison.
              6. Guess the Close-up (pages 47–48): “I think it is a … because it looks … and …”, then one quality and one quantity sentence for the aquarium and the shop.

              Part 4 · Nature, speaking and writing (pages 49–52)

              1. The Eco Map (page 49, Eco Route): air pollution, a dirty river and fewer trees: what happened to the letter, and how to fix it.
              2. My Friendship Podcast (page 50): the four parts of the podcast in order, and he builds a short script he can say out loud.
              3. Write a Letter (pages 51–52): the parts of a friendly letter (date, greeting, opening line, the heart of the message, closing), then he builds his own letter on one of the book's topics.

              Part 5 · Reading on my own (pages 53–54)

              1. The Whispering Forest: the reading passage (retold), questions about it, and the book's “imagine the forest friend” task with describing words.
              2. Grand Review: 10 mixed questions, two from each part, new each time, and a Chapter 4 Letter Champion certificate that shows which part to revisit.

              Notes and small choices

              • Page 34 does not say what is under the cover. Any guess is praised; Speedy then says his own guess (a mailbox) and that it is only a guess.
              • Page 40 Question 2 asks how long each wait felt, which has no single right answer. The game lets him choose, then explains why it felt long. In Question 3 the game counts four answers as right (the story would end quickly, Frog would not keep looking out, the wait would feel short, the letter would come the same day).
              • Open writing tasks (four sentences, two clues, two sentences, the letter, the podcast) are done by choosing and building sentences. Please also let him write them in his book and record the podcast at home as the book asks.
              • Page 55 (The Adventures of Tom Sawyer) looks like a separate class-reader page, so it is not in the game.

              Do it for real

              • Write a short letter to a friend or grandparent and post it together. Count how many days it takes to arrive, just like Toad.
              • Play “I spy” with position words: “I spy something under the table and next to the door.”
              • At dinner, use this, that, these and those: “this spoon” (near), “those cups” (far).
