# English Chapter 4 · The Letter: book notes

Ayansh's English book on Lighthouse Learning (Curriculum → English → "The Letter" flipbook), pages 34–55. The story is adapted from "The Letter" in *Frog and Toad Are Friends* by Arnold Lobel. These notes summarise each page and give the answers used in the game (Snail Mail Trail). The story is summarised, not copied.

## The story (pages 34–38)

| Page | What is on it | Game stop |
|---|---|---|
| 34 | Chapter opener ("Embark"): a picture of something under a blue cover on a desk. "What do you think is under the cover?" (open question, no answer given) | 1 Under the Cover |
| 35 | Toad sits on his porch, sad: it is his "sad time of day", when he waits for the mail, because he never gets any and his mailbox is always empty. Frog thinks someone so nice should get mail, has an idea and hurries home. Word boxes: **porch**, **sighed**, **mailbox** | 2 Toad's Sad Time |
| 36 | Frog writes "Dear Toad, I am glad that you are my best friend. Your best friend, Frog.", puts it in an **envelope** marked "A letter for Toad", and asks a snail to deliver it. The snail goes very slowly. Toad is napping and is tired of waiting for the mail. | 3 Frog's Big Idea |
| 37 | Frog keeps looking out of the window for the snail. Toad says no one will ever send him a letter ("Don't be **silly**"). Frog admits he is waiting because he has sent Toad a letter. | 4 Waiting at the Window |
| 38 | Frog tells Toad what the letter says; Toad thinks it is a very good letter. They wait on the porch together, happy. Four days later the snail arrives and Toad is very **pleased**. Real Route: when we get a message or note, our brain feels happy. | 5 Four Days Later |

## Thinking about the story (pages 39–41)

- **p39 Q1** Tick the character who feels this way: a. sad because the mailbox is empty → **Toad**; b. hopeful while waiting → **Frog**; c. happy after getting the letter → **Toad**.
- **p40 Q2** Colour one circle (very short, short, medium, long, very long) for how long the wait felt: Day 1 waiting for mail; waiting while the snail walks; waiting until the letter arrives. Open: the game lets him choose and then explains why it felt long.
- **p40 Q3** Snail Speed thinking: if the snail was fast, tick what would apply. Counted right in the game: the story would end very quickly; Frog would not keep looking out of the window; the wait would feel short; the letter would arrive the same day. (The book also lists "Toad would feel surprised when the letter comes" and "Toad would stop waiting for the mail"; the game leaves these out and adds two clearly wrong options instead.)
- **p41 Q4** Draw the feeling (sad, worried, happy, hopeful, excited): Toad puts his head down → **sad**; Frog looks out of the window again and again → **hopeful**; Toad smiles when he gets the letter → **happy**.
- **p41 Heritage Route** The evolution of communication: pigeon → letter → telephone → mobile → smartphone.

## Grammar (pages 42–48)

- **p42 Common and proper nouns** word search (11 × 12 grid): colour common nouns yellow and proper nouns green.
  - Across: 1 mangrove forest in India = **SUNDARBANS** (proper), 2 living without fear = **FREEDOM** (common), 3 used to see far objects = **TELESCOPE** (common), 4 a person who travels in space = **ASTRONAUT** (common).
  - Down: 1 very high mountain in India = **HIMALAYAS** (proper), 2 holy river in India = **GANGA** (proper), 3 a large area full of trees = **FOREST** (common), 4 the planet we live on = **EARTH** (proper).
- **p43 Demonstrative pronouns** Write 4 sentences about a garden picture (house, umbrella table, grill, pool, flowers, trees, clouds) using this, that, these, those once each. Open; the game builds four sentences from a garden picture.
- **p44** Read and draw the opposite: a. one thing near the boy → draw one thing far (that); b. one thing far from the girl → draw one thing near (this).
- **p45 Position words** Write two clues about a classroom object using position words. Example: "I am near the teacher and behind the door" (door stopper). Open; the game has riddles and a make-your-own riddle.
- **p45 Adjectives of quality** tell how someone or something is (size, colour, shape, feeling, taste). Examples: a **playful** dolphin, the afternoon feels **quiet**, a **rusty old** bicycle, an **honest** friend.
- **p46 Adjectives of quantity** tell how much or how many: **large numbers**, **some**, **a handful**, **several**. Quality describes, quantity counts or measures. Compare: "A sleepy koala" (quality) / "a lot of plants and trees around the binturongs" (quantity).
- **p47** Complete "I think it is a … because it looks … and …" for close-up drawings (book example: a duck, wet and small; then a tablet corner, a wrinkly eye, a curved handle). The game uses an elephant, an umbrella or a phone.
- **p48** Write 2 sentences with one adjective of quality and one of quantity (example: "The cloudy weather looks gloomy. There are a few people holding umbrellas.") for an aquarium and a supermarket picture.

## Nature, speaking and writing (pages 49–52)

- **p49 Eco Route** Imagine the letter did not reach Toad because something in nature stopped it. Cause–effect map: air pollution, dirty river, fewer trees → what happened to the letter → how to fix it. Think and speak: how does fixing it help nature and people? What action will you take today? Eco message: every problem has a cause, every cause has a solution, and we are part of it.
- **p50 Listening and speaking** Podcast "A Letter from My Friend", 1–2 minutes: say hello and your name, tell a story about your friend, say why you like friendship, end with what your friend means to you. Self-check: name said clearly, shared why friendship matters, spoke confidently. Record at home.
- **p51 Writing** A model friendly letter (Sara to Ali, about a happy day at the park). Guide: date, greeting, opening line, the heart of the message, closing.
- **p52** Write a letter on one topic: something that scares you / how to be brave / what courage means, or to someone you disagree with (a friend who didn't share, a teammate who didn't listen: how you felt and how to fix it).

## Reading (pages 53–54)

- **p53 The Whispering Forest** Amaan follows tiny footprints into the forest behind his house, hears a gentle humming, feels his heart beat fast and leaves a note on a branch for the "forest friend". Q1 What will he see if he follows the footprints? Q2 If the forest could talk, what might it say?
- **p54** Q3 Have you felt curious or scared in a new place? Q4 Imagine the forest friend, draw it and describe it in 2–3 words. (All open.)
