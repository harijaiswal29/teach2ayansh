## Part 1 · The poem (pages 7–10)
- **p7 Embark**: picture of clouds over hills and a river. "Where do you think the clouds are going and why?" (open question)
- **p8 The Rainbow** by Christina Rossetti (1830–1894, public domain):
  > Boats sail on the rivers, / And ships sail on the seas; / But clouds that sail across the sky / Are prettier far than these. //
  > There are bridges on the rivers, / As pretty as you please; / But the bow that bridges heaven, / And overtops the trees, / And builds a road from earth to sky, / Is prettier far than these.
  - Glossary: **sail** to move smoothly over water or through the sky · **prettier** more beautiful · **heaven** the sky or a place high above the earth · **bridges** things that connect two places, often over water · **overtops** goes higher than something else
- **p9 Trail Treasure · About the Poet**: Christina Rossetti, December 5, 1830 – December 29, 1894. Poet, Storyteller, Nature lover, Dreamer. a. Are you an art lover? (Yes/No) b. What is your expression of art? (music / poem / nature) — personal answers.
- **p10**: 1. Read the poem aloud on your own. 2. Practise reading it to a friend or in the mirror. 3a. Where do the boats sail? (on the rivers) 3b. A pair of rhyming words (seas–these, please–trees, seas–please, trees–these) 3c. 'overtops' = goes higher than the trees (options: stands next to / grows from / goes higher than / hides behind) 3d. "And builds a road from earth to sky" in your own words (the rainbow looks like a curved road or bridge from the ground up into the sky) 3e. Another title (open).

## Part 3 · Nouns (pages 13–27)
- **p13 Common and Proper Nouns**: a noun names a person, place, animal or thing. Proper nouns are special names and always start with a capital letter (Tina, Raj, Delhi, Italy, January, August). Common nouns are general names (student, father, mother, dog, city, park, ball, bat).
- **p14** 1. Circus poster: CIRCUS · THE GRAND ARENA · APRIL 7 · ₹350/₹500 · FEATURING THE FABULOUS FLYERS, TITAN THE STRONGMAN, FLAMES OF FURY → list common and proper nouns. 2. Circle the correct noun: a. monkey (not Rahul) b. Ananya c. Hanging garden d. uncle e. Zara.
- **p15** 3. Make two sentences with a proper noun (Mount Everest, Professor Bloom, Amazon River, Tokyo Tower) and a common noun (backpack, telescope, footprints, whistle). **Detour · common mistakes**: mans→men, babys→babies, boxs→boxes, cat's→cats, mouses→mice.
- **p16 Singular and Plural Nouns**: A bird is sitting on the branch / Birds are sitting on the branches. Singular = one (chameleon, park attendant, potato, hero, puppy, book); plural = more than one (chameleons, park attendants, potatoes, heroes, puppies, books).
- **p17** Add **s** (astronaut, bicycle, quail). Ends in **s, ss, sh, ch, x** → add **es** (cactuses, princesses, wishes, matches, foxes). 1. Circle the correct noun for each picture: one astronaut → astronaut; three quails → quails; three fairies → fairies; one volcano → volcano.

- **p21 Countable and Uncountable Nouns**: There are three balloons… / There is some fine dust… Countable: apple, car, book, toy, bird. Uncountable: milk, sugar, water, rice, sand. Numbers with countable (two books, five birds); some, much, a little with uncountable (some rice, a little water).
