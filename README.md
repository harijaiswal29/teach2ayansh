# teach2ayansh

Learning games for Ayansh.

## Times Quest (multiplication, age 7)

Open `index.html` in any browser (phone, tablet or laptop). Progress is saved in that browser.

Five stops, from hands-on to quick recall:

1. **Laddoo Boxes**: fill equal groups and count them. This shows what "×" means.
2. **Froggy Hops**: skip counting on a number line (repeated addition).
3. **Sunflower Garden**: rows × columns. Turning the garden shows 3 × 5 = 5 × 3.
4. **Story Bazaar**: everyday word problems (auto wheels, cricket overs, idli plates).
5. **Rocket Launch**: 10 quick facts from the 2, 5, 10, 3 and 4 tables, with picture hints.

Each stop gives up to 3 stars, and a treasure chart fills in as facts are learned.
There's a "For grown-ups" section at the bottom of the map with tips and offline activities.

## Multiplication Fair (Chapter 9 of the school maths book)

Open `chapter9-fair.html`. It follows the chapter page by page, set at the school fair.

Part 1: tables of 2, 5 and 10

1. **Bakery Trays** (pp. 28–29): spot equal and unequal groups, fix them, then use repeated addition.
2. **Block Towers** (pp. 29–30): towers of 2, 5 and 10; the record table fills in as he guesses each total.
3. **Toy Train** (pp. 31, 35): build the sentence groups × in each group = product.
4. **Lantern Lights** (pp. 32–36): light the tables of 2, 5 and 10, find the patterns, use the "put a zero" trick for tens.
5. **Prize Booth** (pp. 36–37): checkpoint matching, drawing beads, and filling a table row.

Part 2: tables of 3, 4, 6 and zero

6. **Art Club** (p. 38): paint pots and crayons; same trays, 10 in each instead of 5, so the product doubles.
7. **Straw Grid** (pp. 39–41): straws and clay; each straw across adds 3, 4 or 6 meeting points.
8. **Seed Board** (pp. 41–44): tables of 3, 4 and 6 on a Pallanguzhi board.
9. **Hopscotch Lane** (pp. 45–46): traffic lights, tables and insects solved with number-line jumps.
10. **Zero Magic** (p. 47): any number × 0 = 0.

Part 3: tens and ones, and word problems

11. **Golu's Homework** (pp. 48–49): mark Golu's sums right or wrong and pick the fix (spot the error, zero rules).
12. **Table Detective** (pp. 49–51): the eight facts, the × grid from Checkpoint 2, and the hiding-number riddle.
13. **Jumps & Boxes** (pp. 49–51): tap where each jump lands, colour the right boxes, solve the flower-pot garden.
14. **Tens & Ones** (pp. 52–53): vertical multiplication, bundling 10 ones into a ten.
15. **RIDE Problems** (pp. 53–55): Read, Identify, Draw a bar model, Evaluate.
16. **Final Checkpoint** (pp. 55–57): Riya's bar-model mistake, the toy boxes, and the milk calendar.

## Robot Lab (ICT book, Robots chapter)

Open `robot-lab.html`. It follows the Robots chapter (pages 8–15) station by station, with Bolt the lab robot as guide. Every instruction is read aloud (turn off with "Sound on").

Part 1: What is a robot? (pages 8–11)

1. **Robot or Not?** (pp. 8–9): computer brain + moves + sensors = robot. A laptop has a brain but can't walk; a puppy thinks by itself.
2. **Build Bolt** (p. 9): fit the computer brain, sensors, motor and wheels, and battery, each compared to his own body.
3. **Checkpoint 1** (pp. 10–11): questions 1a–1e and the parts-to-jobs matching.
4. **Air Patrol** (p. 11, EcoRoute): a drone's sensors measure smoke, dust and AQI.

Part 2: Robots at work (pages 12–14)

5. **Robot Jobs** (pp. 12–14): sort robots into farm, hospital, fun, rescue, factory and space.
6. **Robot Doctor** (p. 14, Q3): why did the robot vacuum stop?
7. **Robots or People?** (pp. 13–14): who does each job better, and why space robots matter.
8. **My School Robot** (p. 14, Q2): design a robot for school; it then appears on the map.

Part 3: Robots follow instructions (page 15)

9. **Step by Step**: programs are like recipes; put toast, getting ready and the book's robot cycle in order.
10. **Code Bolt**: arrow commands to steer Bolt round a classroom grid, then fix a buggy program.
