## How the chapter games teach

The five chapter games (not Times Quest) share the same teaching pattern:

- **Picture first, then numbers.** Each stop starts with a picture or model before it asks for an answer.
- **Read aloud is on by default.** Every instruction is spoken. The 🗣️ button turns it off, and the game remembers that choice on that device.
- **Help fades.** In the last round of each stop, and in the whole Grand Review, the game shows a gold "On your own!" tag. Number answers are typed on a number pad instead of picked from three choices, and clues only appear after the second wrong try.
- **Each game also has its own practice step:**
  - Multiplication Fair: the first clue is a finger-counting nudge, and the full skip count only comes after that.
  - Tick-Tock Town: in the Straw Clock stop he drags the minute hand round himself and watches the hour hand move.
  - Skip-Hop Park: he says the next number in each group before it lights up, with new real-life rounds (shoes, ₹5 coins, ₹10 notes).
  - Take-Away Bazaar: in the on-your-own rounds he decides when to borrow and taps the digit next door himself.
  - Rangoli Lane: he spots and fixes a broken pattern, and makes up his own number pattern.
