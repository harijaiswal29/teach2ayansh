            For grown-ups

            This follows Chapter 8, “Subtraction of 3-digit Numbers”, in Ayansh's maths book (pages 7–27). Each stop uses the book's own examples and numbers, in the same order. The games teach the ideas behind the sums, not only the answers: blocks for hundreds, tens and ones, why we start at the ones, breaking a ten or a hundred when there are not enough, and adding back to check. A wrong answer costs a little, so three stars means very few slips.

            Part 1 · Take away, no regrouping (pages 7–13)

            1. Block Shop (pages 8–9, Discovery Route): he takes away with place value blocks: 138 − 4, then a 2-digit and a 3-digit number (265 − 32 and 476 − 253). He crosses out cubes, then rods, then flats, and counts what is left.
            2. Expand & Subtract (pages 10–11, Guidepost): 586 − 4, 790 − 50 and 687 − 221, first in expanded form, then in the H T O columns. Ends with the Spotlight: always start from the ones.
            3. Cross Out Blocks (page 12, GRIP-1 Q1): 446 − 5 and 357 − 23.
            4. Expand It (page 12, GRIP-1 Q2): 295 − 22 and 954 − 623.
            5. Line Them Up (page 12, GRIP-1 Q3): 668 − 27, 702 − 102 and 368 − 113. He first says which column the smaller number's digits go in.
            6. Secret Vault (page 13, Checkpoint 1): Detective Thomas's six museum sums. The circled digit of each answer goes into the vault, which opens with the code 306443.

            Part 2 · Regrouping by breaking (pages 14–17)

            1. Break It Up (pages 14–15): he taps a ten rod to break it into 10 ones and a hundred flat to break it into 10 tens. Then 3 tens 2 ones becomes 2 tens 12 ones, and 5 hundreds 4 tens becomes 4 hundreds 14 tens. Spotlight: the number stays the same, only its form changes.
            2. Regroup Guidepost (page 16): 2 hundreds 3 tens = 1 hundred 13 tens, 4 hundreds 5 ones = 3 hundreds 10 tens 5 ones, and 4 hundreds 5 tens = 3 hundreds 14 tens 10 ones.
            3. Fill the Blanks (page 17, GRIP-2): questions a to g.

            Part 3 · Take away with regrouping (pages 17–23)

            1. Not Enough Ones (pages 17 and 19): 462 − 8 with blocks, then 643 − 9 in columns.

            Notes and small choices

            • Checking by adding back (answer + number taken away = starting number) is not in this chapter. I added it as a short last step on some stops because it builds the habit of checking. Each time, he adds and sees that he gets back the number he started with.
            • The book's Discovery Route says “numbers given by your teacher”. I picked 265 − 32 and 476 − 253 for Stop 1.
            • Page 19 has a small misprint: the last column table shows 389 being taken away from 643. The game uses 9, as in the rest of that example.
            • The drawing tasks (page 15 Task 3 and page 18 Task 4) are done by tapping blocks to break them instead of drawing. The comic on page 26 gives no number for the drones; question 2 on page 27 works it out (758).
            • When a wrong digit is tapped in the columns, a dot picture appears (for example 13 dots with 9 crossed out) so he can count what is left.

            Do it for real

            • Play shop with ₹100 notes, ₹10 coins and ₹1 coins as hundreds, tens and ones. When there are not enough ₹1 coins to pay, change a ₹10 coin for ten ₹1 coins. That is regrouping.
            • Page 24: in any story sum, ask him to find the subtraction word first (left, fewer, remain, take away, difference, lost).
            • After any subtraction, ask: “How can you check it?” Add the answer to the number taken away.
