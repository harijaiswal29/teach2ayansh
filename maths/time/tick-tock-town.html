              For grown-ups

              This follows Chapter 10, “Time”, in Ayansh's maths book (pages 58–78). Each stop uses the book's own examples and numbers, in the same order.

              Part 1 · Reading the clock (pages 58–65)

              1. Clock Hands (pages 58–59): he taps the short hand (hour) and the long hand (minute) on the book's 10 o'clock clock, then reads o'clock times. Kuku repeats the book's tip: read the hour hand first.
              2. Set the Clock (page 60): 10 o'clock is 10:00 in digital form. He matches a clock to its digital time, then sets the hands for 11:00, 8:00 and football practice at 5 o'clock.
              3. Straw Clock (page 61): the Discovery Route activity. He moves the long hand one number at a time and counts in 5s up to 6 (30 minutes), sees the short hand sitting between 4 and 5, then finishes the book's challenge (30 more minutes back to 12).
              4. Half Past Pizza (pages 60–62): Radha's pizza cut into two halves, then the book's half past 4, half past 2 and half past 10, plus one more.
              5. Train to Sunnyside (page 63): GRIP-2. Matching 1:30, 7:30 and 11:30 to “half past”, then setting the six clocks where the train stops.
              6. Clock Doctor (pages 64–65): right or wrong? The book's Detour mistake (long hand on 6 is not 6 o'clock) and Sonu's “half past 12” clock (the answer is NO, because the short hand should be halfway between 12 and 1).

              Part 2 · a.m. and p.m. (pages 65–68)

              1. Ayansh's Day (pages 65–66): the book's day table (6:30 wake up to 9:00 bedtime). Each activity goes on a midnight-to-midnight strip as a.m. or p.m.
              2. Sun or Moon? (pages 65 and 67): breakfast and dinner both at 7 o'clock, then all of GRIP-3 (a.m. or p.m., and the most likely time for David, Megha and Rahul).

              Part 3 · How long does it take? (pages 68–70)

              1. One-Minute Challenge (page 68): he writes his name on paper while a 1-minute timer runs, then tells the game how many times. The game can't check this count, so any number is fine.
              2. Minute or Hour? (pages 69–70): the book's examples and GRIP-4, sorted into minutes and hours, plus the Think Route question.

              Part 4 · The calendar (pages 70–73)

              1. Tara's Party (page 70): the October calendar. 2nd Saturday (10), 5 days before (5), one week after (17).
              2. Days & Months (pages 71 and 73): put the days and months in order, GRIP-5, and 365 / 366 days with the leap year.
              3. Rohan's December (pages 71–72): finding the 3rd Thursday (21), counting back 3 days (18) and moving one row down for a week later (28).
              4. January Checkpoint (page 73): Checkpoint question 1, all five parts. Answers: 4 Mondays, Tuesday, 26, 10 weekdays, 11.

              Part 5 · Final checkpoint (pages 74–76)

              1. Picnic Day (page 74): he sets the six picnic clocks from the story, then sorts the times into a.m. and p.m.
              2. Clock Detective (pages 75–76): Ravi's two 6:30 clocks, Aarav in the park (4:30), and the Skill Trail detective clues (11:30 p.m., and 11:30 a.m. in the daytime).
              3. Grand Review: 10 mixed questions from all five parts in a random order, with a Chapter 10 Time Champion certificate that shows which part to revisit.

              Notes and small choices

              • The hands use the book's colours: the long minute hand is red and the short hour hand is blue.
              • When he sets a half past time, the short hand slides halfway to the next number by itself, like a real clock. He taps the number the short hand has just passed.
              • Page 74 says the picnic is on the 2nd Friday of November, but the book shows no November calendar, so the game leaves the date out.
              • Left out: the “Little Clock” poem on page 76 (read it together from the book) and the blank “Mind Movers” note pages 77–78.

              Do it for real

              • Page 61: make a straw clock with a paper plate, a long straw and a short straw. Move the long straw and count in 5s together.
              • Page 64, “My Daily Clock”: write the real times he wakes up, goes to school, has lunch, plays and goes to bed, and say a.m. or p.m. for each.
              • Use your wall calendar: find family birthdays, count “3 days before” and “one week after” by moving one row down.
