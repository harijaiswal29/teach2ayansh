          For grown-ups

          How each stall follows the book

          1. Bakery Trays (pages 28–29): are the groups equal? If not, he fixes them, then adds the equal groups. This introduces the words “repeated addition”.
          2. Block Towers (pages 29–30): the Jodo-block activity. He adds towers of 2, 5 and 10, guesses each new total, and the record table fills in like the one in the book.
          3. Toy Train (pages 31 and 35): he builds the multiplication sentence himself: first number = how many groups, second = how many in each group, answer = the product. “×” is read as “times”.
          4. Lantern Lights (pages 32–36): he lights the tables of 2, 5 and 10 in order, spots the pattern in the last digits, and learns the “count the groups, then put a zero” trick for tens.
          5. Prize Booth (pages 36–37): the checkpoint. Matching repeated addition to multiplication sentences, drawing groups of beads, and filling a table row.

          Part 2 (pages 38–47)

          1. Art Club (page 38): trays of paint pots, then trays of crayons. Same number of trays, but 10 in each instead of 5, so the product doubles (question c). Ends with the five facts from question 3.
          2. Straw Grid (pages 39–41): the straws-and-clay activity. Each straw across adds 3, 4 or 6 new meeting points, and the record table fills in.
          3. Seed Board (pages 41–44): he drops seeds for the tables of 3, 4 and 6 on a Pallanguzhi board, the South Indian counting game on page 41. Each round ends with a pattern question.
          4. Hopscotch Lane (pages 45–46): the book's traffic-light, classroom-table and insect problems, solved with jumps on a number line.
          5. Zero Magic (page 47): empty tables and empty plates show that any number × 0 = 0.

          Part 3 (pages 48–57)

          1. Golu's Homework (pages 48–49): Ayansh is the teacher and marks Golu's sums right or wrong, then picks the correction. It includes the three “Spot the Error” sums from page 49 and the zero rules from page 48 (0 × any number = 0).
          2. Table Detective (pages 49–51): the eight facts from page 49, the × grid from Checkpoint 2, and the Table Detective riddle. The answers are 12 and 16.
          3. Jumps & Boxes (pages 49–51): he taps where each jump lands to show 2 × 6 and 5 × 3, colours the right number of boxes, and solves the flower-pot garden (4 rows of 6).
          4. Tens & Ones (pages 52–53): vertical multiplication. When the answer is 10 or more, he bundles 10 ones into a ten before writing the digits, so “24 ones = 2 tens 4 ones” is something he sees, not just reads.
          5. RIDE Problems (pages 53–55): Read, Identify, Draw a bar model, Evaluate, for the toy shop, flower vases, necklaces and rickshaws.
          6. Final Checkpoint (pages 55–57): Riya's bar-model mistake, the toy boxes of Arav, Sia and Kabir, and his own milk calendar.

          Do it for real

          • If you have Jodo blocks or Lego, build the towers alongside Stall 2 and say the totals out loud.
          • For Stall 7, a few straws (or ice-cream sticks) and a lump of play-dough make the meeting-points activity easy at the dining table.
          • Page 37 asks him to find equal groups around him. At home: shoes in pairs, an egg tray, chairs at the dining table, a strip of tablets. Ask him to say the sentence: “3 groups of 2 make 6, 3 × 2 = 6.”

          For practice beyond this chapter (arrays, number lines and quick facts), there is also Times Quest.
