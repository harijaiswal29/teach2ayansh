            For grown-ups

            This follows Chapter 6, “Patterns”, in Ayansh's maths book (pages 47–53). Each stop uses the book's own examples, in the same order. The games teach the ideas, not only the answers: a pattern is something that repeats in a regular way, we name the repeating part with letters (A, B, C), we find what comes next by looking back one repeat, and a number pattern adds or takes away the same number each time. A wrong answer costs a little, so three stars means very few slips.

            Part 1 · Patterns in shapes and colours (pages 47–50)

            1. Patterns Everywhere (pages 47–48): the rangoli from the chapter opener, the wall, dress and chessboard (he finishes a chessboard row), then he picks which row repeats in a regular way.
            2. Name It with Letters (pages 48–49, Guidepost): blue and yellow circles (ABAB), triangles and ovals (AABB), red, blue and green rectangles (ABC). He gives each shape its letter: the same shape gets the same letter, a new shape gets a new letter.
            3. What's My Pattern? (page 49, Discovery Route): he picks 2 shapes and builds a repeating pattern, then 3 shapes, then the “trickier” one: the same shape in two colours.
            4. Missing Shape (page 50, GRIP-1): a. the missing yellow circle (ABC) and b. the missing triangle (ABB). Round 3 (AAB) is extra practice I added.

            Part 2 · Patterns in art (pages 50–51)

            1. Thumb-print Parade: Ruhi's cat and cow prints, the stamp activity (stamp 1, stamp 2, stamp 1, stamp 2), turning the odd stamps into one creature and the even stamps into another, and “Which type of pattern is this?” (ABAB).

            Part 3 · Patterns in numbers (pages 51–52)

            1. Number Hops (pages 51–52, Guidepost): Patterns 1 to 5, adding 2, 5 and 10, and subtracting 2 and 5. He sees the first three numbers, finds the jump, then gives the next two.
            2. Fill the Gaps (page 52, GRIP-2): a. 12, 15 b. 15, 10 c. 12, 20 d. 30, 10.
            3. Pattern Hunt (page 52, Real Route): “Where do you see a pattern in your home?” as a tap-the-patterns hunt in two rooms.

            Part 4 · Checkpoint (pages 52–53)

            1. Colour the Grid (page 52, Checkpoint 1): the 6 × 6 colour grid with six empty boxes.
            2. Sam's Tower (page 53, Checkpoint 2): Day 4 = 8 blocks, Day 5 = 10 blocks, 30 blocks in all.
            3. Madhubani Border (page 53, Trail Treasure): a fish, flower and diamond border to finish and name (ABC).
            4. Grand Review: 10 mixed questions from all four parts, new each time, and a Chapter 6 Pattern Champion certificate that shows which part to revisit.

            Notes and small choices

            • Checkpoint 1 does not say what the rule is. I read it as: every row is the row above slid one box to the right, so each slanting line is one colour. The answers are row 1 orange, row 2 green, row 4 red and blue, row 6 pink and yellow. The game teaches it by lighting up the slanting line.
            • The Real Route question is open (“Where do you see pattern in your home?”). I turned it into a hunt with home things I drew. Please also ask him the question for real.
            • The Discovery Route uses Rangometry pieces in class; the game uses drawn shapes. In the stamp activity he chooses his own creatures.
            • The book shows all five numbers in Patterns 1 to 5. The game hides the last two so he works them out.
            • I did not open the separate GRIP and Checkpoint PDFs on the dashboard; I assumed they repeat the book pages.

            Do it for real

            • Make a sound pattern: clap, snap, clap, snap (AB), or clap, clap, stamp (AAB). Ask him to name it with letters.
            • String beads or line up spoons and forks in a pattern and ask what comes next.
            • Walk up the stairs counting in 2s, then come down counting back in 2s.
