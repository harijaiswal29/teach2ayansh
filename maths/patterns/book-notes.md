p47 Opener (Embark): woman making a rangoli at a door. "What do you notice about the beautiful rangoli on the floor?"
p48 "Patterns are all around us … in nature, in our homes and even in the clothes we wear." Pattern on the wall (bricks), on the dress (red/white zigzag), on the chessboard. Section "Patterns in Shapes and Colours": shapes and colours repeated in a regular way. Guidepost: blue circle, yellow circle … name blue A, yellow B → A B A B A B, an ABAB pattern. Two green triangles, two orange ovals … → AA BB AA BB, an AABB pattern.
p49 Red, blue, green rectangle repeated → A B C A B C, an ABC pattern. Discovery Route "What's My Pattern?" (Rangometry shapes, group work): pick 2 shapes and make a repeating pattern; then 3 shapes; trickier: same shape in two different colours, does it still make a pattern?
p50 GRIP-1: draw the missing shape and name the pattern with letters. a. yellow circle, green triangle, red circle, ___, triangle, red circle → yellow circle; ABC. b. green triangle, red circle, red circle, ___, red circle, red circle → triangle; ABB. Section "Patterns in Art": Ruhi made a pattern with thumb and finger prints (cat, cow, cat, cow). Activity: thumb stamp colour 1, other thumb colour 2, keep repeating stamp 1, stamp 2 …
p51 Activity cont.: turn odd stamps (1,3,5) into one creature, even stamps (2,4,6) into another. "Which type of pattern is this?" AAB / ABAB / ABCABC → ABAB. Section "Patterns in Numbers": "When you add or subtract the same number each time, you get a number pattern." Guidepost: Pattern 1 adding 2: 2,4,6,8,10. Pattern 2 adding 5: 5,10,15,20,25. Pattern 3 adding 10: 10,20,30,40,50.
p52 Pattern 4 subtracting 2: 20,18,16,14,12. Pattern 5 subtracting 5: 25,20,15,10,5.
